# MiniMax H3 - Rapid Lora Training with Fizgig
**Source Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Sems-_CMQE

---

## Executive Summary
This video provides a detailed, practical walkthrough on how to train a Lora for the MiniMax H3 model using the Fizgig application, and subsequently how to deploy it within ComfyUI. The creator uses a "non-optimal" dataset consisting of screenshots from *The Fifth Element* to demonstrate that high-quality, recognizable character likenesses can be achieved without perfect source material or exhaustive training times. The tutorial emphasizes that in the context of Fizgig's still previews for video models, likeness is the primary metric to watch rather than overall image quality. It challenges conventional training wisdom by recommending rank 8/alpha 8 settings over the traditional 16/16, advocating for smaller, more agile file sizes that handle faster learning rates better. The video also highlights the power of modern diffusion models by showcasing how a Lora trained on low-resolution (0.25 megapixels) '90s film stills can still generate highly detailed, high-resolution outputs. Ultimately, the guide is a testament to rapid iteration, showing that significant progress can be seen in just a few hundred steps, and encourages users to experiment with fast presets and pausing training early to test in ComfyUI.

## Chronological Chapter Breakdown

### Introduction and Dataset Context
The video begins by outlining the objective: demonstrating rapid Lora training for MiniMax H3 in Fizgig and utilizing the result in ComfyUI, with a target runtime of under 20-25 minutes. The creator emphasizes the use of a "very non-optimal dataset"—specifically, a collection of screenshots featuring Chris Tucker's character from a single scene in the movie *The Fifth Element*. This approach highlights the robustness of the training process, showing that pristine studio-quality images are not strictly necessary to achieve a strong character likeness.

### Image Preparation and Captioning Setup
The creator briefly touches upon the image preparation and captioning tabs in Fizgig, noting that these topics were covered more extensively in a previous tutorial. For this session, the 29 images in the dataset have already been detailed and captioned using the Qwen model (specifically, the Qwen model bundled with Korea 2, which can be auto-downloaded). The focus remains on moving quickly into the training phase, relying on these pre-generated, detailed captions to guide the MiniMax model.

### Configuring Training Settings
A critical part of the tutorial involves configuring the training settings for MiniMax H3. The creator notes that MiniMax is primarily a video model, but advocates for using stills for captions during training. While video options exist, they significantly slow down the process and cause excessive memory swapping. The creator stresses a key concept: the still previews generated during training are useful for tracking the progression of *likeness*, but they are not an accurate representation of the final *quality*. Previews may look like "horrific monstrosities," yet the exact same epoch loaded into ComfyUI can produce great results. A sample caption from Qwen is grabbed to use as the sample prompt, and the resolution is set to 1024x1024.

### The Fast Preset and Rank/Alpha Optimization
In the training tab, the creator selects the "fast preset," noting that it took days of experimentation to find the optimal settings because MiniMax H3 is "pretty awkward to train." A significant tip provided is to use rank 8 and alpha 8, contrary to the older standard of 16/16 or 32/32. The creator explains that with modern models containing vastly more parameters, 16/16 is unnecessary for achieving deep character quality. Rank 8/alpha 8 results in a smaller, more agile Lora file that can handle faster learning rates. The target megapixels are set low (0.25) to achieve "crazy good results."

### Monitoring the Training Process
The training process is initiated, with the creator explaining that Fizgig uses the best configuration possible for the user's system to keep VRAM usage manageable. As the training progresses through the early epochs (like epoch 1), the generated previews are shown. The creator reiterates that while these early previews look "horrendous," they indicate that the model is barely moving. The strategy is to monitor the likeness progression, not the aesthetic quality of the stills.

### Pausing and Testing Early Epochs
The video demonstrates a practical workflow: pausing the training mid-way (around 400-500 steps, or epoch 16) to test the Lora in ComfyUI. The creator explains that by pausing, Fizgig releases the VRAM, allowing the user to load ComfyUI without closing Fizgig. The epoch 16 Lora is downloaded and placed into the appropriate ComfyUI models directory. A quick test prompt ("A man... walking towards me. They're wearing purple... and he comes right up to the camera and screams") is run to evaluate the early results.

### Comparing Epochs in ComfyUI
In ComfyUI, the creator sets up a basic workflow using a default template with the newly trained Lora. An initial mistake involving the wrong Lora loader is corrected. The epoch 16 Lora is tested, producing a rapid 5-second generation. The creator then jumps forward in time to show the final epoch (epoch 40, over 1,100 steps) for comparison. Surprisingly, the epoch 16 result is deemed "kind of just as good" as the final epoch, underscoring the efficiency of the fast training approach.

### High-Resolution Upscaling Demonstration
In a final demonstration of the diffusion model's capabilities, the creator goes back to the epoch 16 Lora and sets up a 1-second generation at a significantly higher resolution (1080x1920, roughly 1.5 megapixels). Despite being trained on low-resolution (0.25 megapixel) source material that had an early '90s film look, the model generates an output with "fantastic" detail. This highlights the powerful generalization and upscaling abilities inherent in the current generation of tools.
