This documentary-style tutorial provides an in-depth, hands-on walkthrough of creating highly engaging, rapidly produced "microdramas" using artificial intelligence. What distinguishes them from traditional serialized television is not just their brevity—usually lasting only 60 to 90 seconds—but their aggressive narrative pacing. They are engineered to deliver constant dopamine hits through relentless plot twists, melodramatic scenarios, and cliffhangers at the end of nearly every minute.

The economic scale of this format is staggering. The narrator reveals that this specific video format is currently generating approximately $11 billion annually. This massive revenue stream is fueled by a narrative structure that relies on tropes that, while perhaps lacking in highbrow literary merit, are undeniably effective at retaining viewer attention. The focus is squarely on creating an addictive viewing experience, utilizing themes such as secret billionaires, fake marriages, sudden betrayals, and elaborate revenge arcs. The primary objective is to compel the audience to immediately swipe to or click on the next episode.

Historically, despite their short runtime and fast-paced release schedules, commercially produced microdramas have still required traditional filmmaking elements. They necessitate hiring real actors, scouting and securing physical sets, and employing full production crews, all of which incur significant costs and logistical challenges. However, the core premise of this tutorial is to demonstrate that this traditional, labor-intensive model is on the brink of a massive paradigm shift. The creator asserts that a substantial portion of this multi-billion dollar industry is about to be completely overhauled by artificial intelligence, democratizing the production process and allowing solo creators to generate entire seasons of content from their desks in a matter of minutes.

In traditional television production, a show bible is a foundational document that outlines the series' universe, character biographies, overall narrative arcs, and stylistic guidelines, ensuring consistency across episodes and seasons, especially when multiple writers or directors are involved. The creator has adapted this concept for the AI age. This digital "Bible" is a pre-configured file that is fed directly into the AI agent. By uploading this Bible, the creator effectively programs the AI agent with a "prompting style" and a deep understanding of the project's parameters. This step is crucial because it transforms the AI from a generic chatbot into a specialized, collaborative production assistant. Instead of having to repeatedly instruct the AI on how to format a storyboard or what aspect ratio to use, the agent absorbs these rules from the Bible, streamlining the workflow and allowing the human creator to focus on higher-level creative decisions rather than tedious, repetitive prompting.

### 3. Character Consistency and Visual Development

A successful drama, regardless of its length, relies heavily on its characters. In the realm of AI-generated video, achieving character consistency has traditionally been a significant hurdle. If a character looks completely different in every shot, the narrative illusion is broken, and the audience disengages. The tutorial addresses this challenge head-on by demonstrating a systematic approach to character design using AI.

The process begins not with generating scenes, but with establishing "character reference sheets." The creator starts by uploading a single photo of himself to serve as the visual basis for the main character, "Zeus." The AI agent, using the structural guidelines provided in the previously uploaded Bible, then generates a comprehensive character sheet. This isn't just a single image, but a reference document that locks in the character's appearance, ensuring that subsequent video generations will recognize and accurately reproduce those specific facial features, clothing styles, and overall aesthetic.

Crucially, the workflow is designed for efficiency. Once the initial character is established and approved, the creator instructs the AI agent to generate character sheets for the rest of the cast—including the "cruel stepmother," the "villainish" sister, and another antagonist—in parallel. The agent automatically pulls the required prompt structures from the Bible, eliminating the need for the human user to manually write complex prompts for each individual character. This parallel generation showcases the scalability of the AI pipeline, allowing an entire cast to be visually designed and locked in within minutes. The creator emphasizes the stark contrast required in microdramas, noting the need for distinct visual differences between "good" and "bad" characters to instantly communicate their roles to the audience.

### 4. Storyboarding and Pacing: The Architecture of the Episode

Once the characters are visually established, the workflow transitions from design to narrative construction through the process of storyboarding. In the context of microdramas, where every second is crucial for viewer retention, pacing is arguably the most important element. The creator explains that a typical episode, lasting around 100 seconds, requires meticulous planning to ensure the emotional beats hit correctly and the story advances at the necessary rapid clip. To achieve this, the AI agent is instructed to break down the synopsis into specific acts and generate visual storyboards for each.

The creator utilizes a specific "three times three storyboard grid" method, yielding a total of four storyboard panels for a single episode. This structure provides a clear visual map of the episode's progression before any final rendering occurs. What makes this AI-driven storyboarding process particularly advanced is its integration of dialogue. The creator notes that they added a new variable to their "Bible" which ensures that dialogue lines are built directly into each storyboard panel. This means the storyboard isn't just a collection of images; it's a complete blueprint containing the visual composition, the emotional context, and the script, all tightly integrated.

This phase is essential for quality control. It allows the creator to review the narrative flow, verify that the pacing is "natural and not rushed," and ensure that the crucial "emotional and dialogue-driven scenes" are mapped out effectively. By reviewing these storyboards, the creator can make adjustments to the story's trajectory before committing processing power to video generation, a step that is both computationally expensive and time-consuming. The storyboards serve as the architectural plans; once they are approved, the actual construction (video generation) can proceed with confidence.

### 5. Final Generation and the Future of Binge-Ready Content

The culmination of the workflow is the generation of the final video clips and their subsequent assembly into a cohesive episode. Following the approval of the storyboards, the creator instructs the AI to proceed with the video generation. To maximize efficiency, this process is done in parallel. The AI agent generates multiple clips simultaneously, utilizing the designated video generation model (referred to as "Seedance 2") while referencing the established character sheets and storyboards. The creator highlights specific, highly dramatic beats generated during this phase, such as the "famous micro drama slap" and the "identity reveal," confirming that the AI successfully captured the melodramatic tone required by the genre.

A key feature of the InVideo Agent One workflow is its automated post-production capability. The creator notes that, without prompting, the agent takes the individual generated clips and stitches them together into a "slate state," effectively assembling the final, continuous microdrama episode. This resulting video is then presented in a "notebook" interface, which serves as a centralized hub for all project assets. This interface allows the creator to view the episode outline, the individual storyboards, and the final video in a single, organized view, facilitating easy review and editing.

Looking toward the future, the creator emphasizes the scalability of this method. They demonstrate how this exact workflow can be seamlessly extended to create multi-episode seasons. By initiating a "parallel agent" for episode two, the new agent inherits the entire context—the character sheets, the established lore, and the events of the first episode—ensuring perfect continuity. The creator ends by previewing the capabilities of the upcoming "Seedance 2.5" model, predicting that its ability to utilize numerous reference points and generate longer, highly consistent clips will make it the definitive tool for episodic short dramas, signaling that the AI production revolution is only just beginning.
