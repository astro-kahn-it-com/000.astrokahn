## 6-Word Premise

Get AI Trash Out Of Cinema

## Chronological Chapter Breakdown

### 1. The Perceived "Inevitability" of AI in Hollywood
The video opens by tracing the rapid infiltration of generative AI into the film industry over the past four years. Moving beyond viral oddities like the "Will Smith eating spaghetti" video or Wes Anderson style parodies, the speaker notes how AI is now being embraced by major studios and even acclaimed filmmakers. The most shocking news to the speaker, however, is Fable Studios' plan to use AI to "finish" Orson Welles's mutilated masterpiece, *The Magnificent Ambersons*.

> **"Generative AI has been shoved into virtually every aspect of our lives across the last 4 years... And as much as I personally hate all of this, none of these affected me quite as much as the news that an AI filmmaking studio called Fable was planning to use AI to complete The Magnificent Ambersons."**

*Deep Analysis:* The introduction establishes the stakes: AI is no longer just a novelty; it is actively attempting to insert itself into high-art cinema. By framing AI's creep as being "shoved" into our lives, the speaker immediately challenges the tech industry narrative that AI is a natural, inevitable progression of art. It sets up the core conflict between tech executives pushing AI for its own sake and cinephiles trying to protect the artistic integrity of film history.

### 2. The Tragedy of The Magnificent Ambersons
The speaker provides crucial historical context for *The Magnificent Ambersons*. Following *Citizen Kane*, Welles created an ambitious, visually stunning epic that studio RKO panicked over after a poor test screening. While Welles was in South America, RKO drastically re-edited the film, removing 43 minutes of footage and replacing his original ending with a happy one. The missing footage was destroyed, becoming a legendary "holy grail" of cinema.

> **"The excised footage was stored for a time by RKO, but eventually destroyed to clear up storage space. Since then, the missing footage has become one of the great holy grails of cinematic history, a legendary artifact lost to time..."**

*Deep Analysis:* This chapter underscores why *Ambersons* is so revered and why touching it is controversial. The film is already defined by corporate interference destroying an artist's vision. By explaining the tragic history of the film, the speaker highlights the bitter irony of Fable Studios—another corporate entity—now attempting to impose *their* technological vision onto the remains of Welles's work under the guise of "completing" it.

### 3. Fable Studios' True Motivations
The speaker questions *why* Fable chose this specific, niche film. Unlike a popular spectacle like *The Wizard of Oz*, *Ambersons* only appeals to hardcore cinephiles—the very people most likely to hate AI manipulation. The speaker argues the true goal isn't artistic rescue, but using the film's detailed remaining blueprints (cutting continuities, storyboards) as a crutch for AI that cannot generate good art independently.

> **"It offers a blueprint for the AI to follow. Generative AI needs a ton of guidance to produce anything even remotely compelling... In short, Ambersons is the perfect vehicle for demonstrating AI's worth, offering detailed instructions for the AI to follow..."**

*Deep Analysis:* This is the crux of the speaker's argument against the project. Generative AI is inherently uncreative and produces "slop" when left to its own devices. *Ambersons* is appealing to Fable not just out of cinephile reverence, but because Welles did the creative heavy lifting decades ago. Fable intends to use Welles's genius to mask the creative bankruptcy of their own AI tools.

### 4. Digital Necromancy and Ethical Lapses
The ethical issues of the project are brought into sharp focus. Fable is using AI to replicate the voices and faces of dead actors who cannot consent to this usage. Furthermore, Fable admitted they did not even consult the Orson Welles estate or his daughter, Beatrice, before beginning the project, mirroring the tech industry's standard "ask forgiveness, not permission" ethos.

> **"...manipulating the performance of long dead actors is actually kind of sus, not going to lie. Well, you didn't consult her and you're still engaging in a digital necromancy you yourself admit is indefensible. So, clearly, you don't actually care."**

*Deep Analysis:* The speaker eviscerates the tech-bro defense that AI is simply an "inevitable" future. By pointing out the blatant disregard for consent and the aggressive, uninvited nature of the project, the speaker reveals the predatory nature of Fable's actions. The concept of "digital necromancy" highlights the profound disrespect of puppeteering the dead for a modern tech demo.

### 5. AI Cannot Replace Human Performance
Even if Fable had the best intentions and perfect documentation, the speaker argues they cannot recreate the film because the original performances are gone. An AI can map a face or mimic a voice, but it cannot generate the nuanced, human performance of actors like Joseph Cotton or Agnes Moorehead. AI is fundamentally incapable of replacing the human element of cinema.

> **"Fable's AI might be able to create a photorealistic likeness of the actors or mimic their voices... but it still won't be their performances. Just like how Peter Cushing didn't give a performance in Rogue One... A different anonymous actor gave a performance, which was then run through a program..."**

*Deep Analysis:* This chapter defends the soul of acting against technological simulation. The speaker makes a vital distinction between a "likeness" and a "performance." Fable's project is not a reconstruction; it is a remake masquerading as the original. The AI is simply a digital mask worn by anonymous modern actors, severing the connection between the original artist and the art.

### 6. The Quest for Artistic Legitimacy
The video concludes by analyzing Fable's ultimate goal: artistic legitimacy for generative AI. AI is currently associated with low-quality slop, memes, and grifts. By associating their technology with a revered master like Orson Welles, Fable hopes to elevate the perceived value of AI-generated content, turning a cheap tech stunt into a respected artistic endeavor.

> **"Because the goal isn't to complete The Magnificent Ambersons. It's to use The Magnificent Ambersons to increase the perceived value of AI-generated content... It is ultimately a self-serving lie that should be rejected."**

*Deep Analysis:* The speaker unmasks the entire project as a cynical PR campaign. Fable doesn't want to save Orson Welles; they want Orson Welles to save *them* from their reputation as purveyors of tech-slop. The video ends with a plea to appreciate the ruined, incomplete version of *Ambersons* as it is—a testament to human artistry, flaws and all—rather than accepting a sterile, AI-generated fake.

## Conclusion & Takeaways

Fable Studios' attempt to complete *The Magnificent Ambersons* using generative AI is a deeply cynical endeavor disguised as artistic restoration. By leveraging the detailed blueprints left behind by Orson Welles, Fable seeks to use his genius as a crutch to mask the creative limitations of their AI tools. The project is marred by profound ethical issues, including "digital necromancy"—puppeteering deceased actors without consent—and a blatant disregard for the Welles estate. Ultimately, AI can mimic voices and faces, but it cannot replicate the human soul of a performance. This endeavor is not about saving cinema history; it is a self-serving attempt by the tech industry to gain unearned artistic legitimacy for generative AI by grafting it onto the legacy of a true cinematic master. True cinephiles should reject this digital forgery and embrace the beautiful, human ruins of the original 1942 cut.
