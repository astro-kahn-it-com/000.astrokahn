This video explores how writers forcing predefined "agendas" onto their characters and plots can irreparably damage a story's natural logic, using explicit examples from *House of the Dragon* and *Game of Thrones* to illustrate the failure of artificial narrative contortions.

## Storytelling Agendas Disrupt Natural Narrative Progression

## Chronological Chapter Breakdown

### The Definition and Danger of Storytelling Agendas
The video introduces the concept of "agendas" in storytelling, defining it as a phenomenon where a writer decides upon a specific outcome, archetype, or role for a character before the narrative has organically justified it. While having a predefined path is not inherently detrimental if the character and world are constructed to support it from the start, it becomes a severe issue in long-form narratives or adaptations. When a story's established logic clashes with the writer's newly imposed agenda—such as forcing two characters to fall in love or become enemies without proper buildup—the narrative is forced to contort unnaturally. This disjointed execution makes the writer's hand painfully obvious to the audience, pulling them out of the fiction and highlighting the artificiality of the plot.

> **"When I say agendas, I'm referring to the phenomenon where a writer wants something to be true in their story, so they force the story in the certain patterns. This character is the hero is an agenda. You're saying before you even write the story that you know what role this character plays."**

*Deep Analysis:* The foundational problem with storytelling agendas is the disruption of cause and effect. In a well-constructed narrative, character actions and plot developments arise organically from previous events and established motivations. When an agenda is prioritized, this chain is broken. The writer operates from the conclusion backward, often ignoring the established parameters of their own world. This top-down approach to writing reduces characters from autonomous, believable entities into mere vehicles for plot advancement. The audience's immersion relies on the illusion that the characters are driving the story through their choices; when an agenda dictates their actions, that illusion shatters, replacing emotional investment with a cynical awareness of the writer's mechanical manipulation. It transforms the narrative from an exploration of truth into a rigid exercise in fulfilling a predetermined checklist.

### The Alicent Hightower Problem in House of the Dragon
The video uses Alicent Hightower from *House of the Dragon* as a primary case study of an agenda gone wrong. In the first season, Alicent was established as a deeply political player, fiercely protective of her family, and heavily involved in the machinations of the court. Her storyline was rich and logically grounded. However, in the second season, her natural role in the narrative diminished due to the death of the king and her children coming of age. Instead of allowing her character to naturally transition into a background role reflecting her loss of power, the writers forced an agenda to maintain her status as a co-protagonist alongside Rhaenyra. To justify her continued central presence, the story unnaturally contorts, leading her to betray her own family—a direct contradiction to her fiercely loyal characterization in the first season—simply to create dramatic friction and elevate her narrative importance.

> **"The show unnaturally props up Alicent when logically in the story, she should have less agency, less to do. In the name of this agenda, they have Alicent to betray her own family so Rhaenyra can win. [...] Making Alicent team black is maybe the worst thing the show has done. It's a bizarre choice that is clearly driven by what someone thinks the story should be as opposed to what it is."**

*Deep Analysis:* The mishandling of Alicent Hightower illustrates the danger of the "importance agenda," where a character's screen time and narrative weight are prioritized over their logical trajectory. The writers clearly envisioned a specific dynamic—two powerful women on opposite sides of a war—and forced that dynamic to exist regardless of the established character arcs. By having Alicent abandon her previously established fierce familial loyalty (even to the point of endangering her son), the show sacrifices internal consistency for the sake of thematic symmetry. This betrays the audience's investment in the character's earlier development. True character evolution requires a logical bridge between states; jumping from fiercely protective mother to treasonous collaborator without adequate psychological groundwork exposes the writers' desperate need to fulfill their agenda, ultimately cheapening the character and the political stakes of the conflict.

### The Collapse of Character Logic in Game of Thrones
The analysis expands to *Game of Thrones*, citing its notoriously divisive ending as a prime example of shifting agendas destroying narrative coherence. The video highlights Daenerys Targaryen, whose sudden pivot from a just liberator to a mass murderer in the penultimate episode felt entirely unearned. The speaker suggests that the writers clung to the "hero agenda" for Daenerys for far too long, failing to adequately lay the groundwork for her eventual heel turn. Consequently, when the predetermined endpoint (her burning King's Landing) arrived, it felt like an abrupt and shocking betrayal of the character rather than a tragic, inevitable conclusion. Similarly, the video mentions Tyrion Lannister, noting that the writers' commitment to keeping him as a "funny advisor" stripped away the darker, more complex trajectory his character was naturally on course to explore.

> **"Game of Thrones had a shocking ending. It was shocking because its agendas very rapidly shifted. The character of Daenerys, for example, was a just liberator for most of her run. She was running on an agenda of heroism. [...] they just held onto the hero agenda for too long before suddenly dropping it."**

*Deep Analysis:* The *Game of Thrones* examples demonstrate the catastrophic consequences of mismanaged pacing in relation to long-term agendas. When a creator knows the endpoint but delays the necessary developmental steps to reach it—either to preserve a character's popularity or due to a lack of planning—the final execution becomes a jarring leap rather than a smooth transition. Daenerys's arc required a slow, meticulous erosion of her moral compass, but the show prioritized the immediate gratification of her heroic moments until the very end. This reveals a fundamental misunderstanding of narrative structure: the journey is just as important as the destination. An agenda is useless if the path leading to it is broken. By forcefully realigning characters to their predetermined endpoints at the last possible second, the showrunners sacrificed organic storytelling for shock value, proving that an inflexible agenda can retroactively ruin years of careful character building.

### Stripping Away Agendas to Rejuvenate Stories
In the final section, the speaker offers a solution for writers trapped by their own agendas: relentlessly questioning the necessity of every narrative element. By asking questions like "Does this character have to be in this episode?" or "Do these characters have to end up together?", writers can break free from the "fossilized" constraints they have unconsciously imposed upon their work. Stripping away these artificial requirements allows the story to "breathe" and natural narrative pathways to open up. The speaker emphasizes that many writers remain stuck in mediocrity because they refuse to challenge their foundational assumptions about what their story *must* be. By entering a state of fluidity and being willing to completely alter or abandon predetermined concepts, writers can discover superior, organic storylines that were previously hidden behind their rigid agendas.

> **"Thus, we strip those agendas away, and now the story can breathe. It could go this way, it could go that way, and most likely a better storyline will reveal itself. This is how you write a twist, or rather, how you set the scene for twists to present themselves. Just by freeing yourself from those stale agendas."**

*Deep Analysis:* This advice targets the psychological core of the writer's block: the fear of abandoning control. Agendas often function as safety nets, providing a false sense of security in the chaotic process of creation. However, this safety comes at the cost of vitality. By advocating for the removal of these constraints, the speaker encourages a more improvisational, character-driven approach to storytelling. When a writer stops dictating the plot and starts listening to the logical implications of their own world-building, the story gains a sense of autonomy. Twists and developments that arise from this method feel profound and inevitable because they are rooted in the established reality of the narrative, rather than imposed from above. This process of continuous re-evaluation and willingness to dismantle one's own structural assumptions is the hallmark of mature, responsive writing. It requires the humility to accept that the initial concept may not be the best version of the story.

## Conclusion & Takeaways

The video provides a compelling critique of "storytelling agendas"—the practice of forcing a narrative to conform to a predetermined outcome regardless of its established internal logic. The central thesis is that when writers prioritize what they *want* to happen over what *logically should* happen based on character development and world-building, they inevitably damage the story's integrity. By analyzing specific failures in major productions like *House of the Dragon* and *Game of Thrones*, the speaker demonstrates how forcing importance, relationships, or shocking twists onto characters without proper groundwork leads to jarring contortions that alienate the audience.

Ultimately, the video advocates for a more fluid, responsive approach to writing. By critically examining their foundational assumptions and asking "Does it have to be that way?", writers can dismantle the rigid, "fossilized" agendas trapping their work. Stripping away these artificial constraints allows characters to act according to their true natures and the story to "breathe," fostering an environment where natural, organic plot progressions and genuinely earned twists can emerge.
