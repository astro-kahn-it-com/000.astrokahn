### 2. World-Building: The Explicit vs. The Artful Gap
Sloan discusses the different approaches to world-building in fiction, contrasting the exhaustive detail of writers like Tolkien with the "artful gaps" left by others to engage the reader's imagination.

> **"There's the implicit or the sort of art, the world building of artful gaps... the world there feels more than rich. It feels almost psychedelic. Um, and it's completely captivating."**

*Deep Analysis:* The discussion reveals a profound insight into the mechanics of storytelling. While meticulous world-building (the "explicit" approach) provides a satisfyingly complete universe, the "implicit" approach—relying on artful gaps—invites the reader to co-create the world. This technique, admired by Sloan in authors like Ursula K. Le Guin and M. John Harrison, requires a delicate balance. It suggests that leaving space for the reader's imagination can result in a more immersive and emotionally resonant experience than detailing every historical or geographical fact.

*Deep Analysis:* Sloan elevates the physical act of reading from a simple consumption of text to a sensory ritual heavily dependent on environment. By describing reading as the "empire of light," he underscores the intimacy and focus required to engage with fiction. The "bubble of light" represents a sanctuary away from the chaotic world, an artificial but necessary condition that allows the imagination to flourish. This perspective highlights the enduring power of physical books and the specific atmospheric conditions that enhance human connection to stories.
